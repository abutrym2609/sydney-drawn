# Sydney: From Space to Place — illustration and story review

Reviewed 7 September 2026. Creative project Claude + GPT + Gemini, curated by Anastasia Butrym.

All 106 current illustrations were visually inspected against their stories, including the accepted Glebe image with the new Sydney Fish Market. Every story now contains ten distinct illustrated details, an introduction and an individual slogan: 1,060 details in total. Narration uses We/us/our throughout; the title of Newtown's “I Have a Dream” mural is preserved.

The review covers correspondence between pictures and prose, significant omissions, geographical compression and targeted checks of changing projects and historical claims. It is an editorial review, not a claim that every historical statement has been independently verified against archival records. Dates and construction statuses below reflect the review date. The ten entries explain the strongest available elements of each existing picture; they do not turn an unpictured landmark into a pictured one.

## Illustration revision completed — 7 September 2026

The 19 unique suburbs in the two priority tables below have now been redrawn, together with Glebe, where Anastasia requested a greyhound. All 20 replacement images were visually inspected and their ten-part stories were synchronized with the resulting artwork. The accepted new Sydney Fish Market remains in Glebe. About now explains Anastasia’s recent arrival in Sydney and how her personal exploration became an invitation to other readers.

The two tables below preserve the **original, now completed priority requests**. Their wording describes the earlier artwork. The 106-suburb table further down records the updated state. Suggestions for other suburbs remain editorial possibilities rather than completed changes.

These remain surreal compositions, with deliberately compressed geography. They are not architectural surveys. In particular, the Waverton engraving follows a documentary photograph but is not an archaeological tracing; the airport’s remote control room is brought close to its camera mast as a visual explanation; the Parramatta theatre is the existing Riverside, not a claim that its future replacement is built.
